Who makes this

I'm Gil. I kept struggling to find out what's on in Amsterdam. The big agendas are mostly paid placements, and the small independent and community stuff is scattered over dozens of sites, often only in Dutch.

So I put it all in one place. It's for everyone, but mostly for people who live here or are here for a while, not tourists. English can be a struggle, so I mark what works in English.

I have no financial interest in this. No ads, no tickets, nothing sold. I hope you find it useful, and you can always get in touch.

Everything here belongs to the venues and organisers. I just collect it and link back, and I'm not affiliated with any of them. Details can be wrong, so check with the venue. If you run a page and want off, use the form and pick "Take something down".

How it works

Twice a day the site checks the public pages of 70 venues and listing sites, like a person going through them one by one. It picks out the title, date, time, place and link, sorts events into types with a keyword list (say, "techno" means Club), and puts it all on one page.

Nothing is made up. Every event links to where it came from.

AI statement

The whole site was built with AI. I direct it, make every choice and check the results, but I didn't write the code. I'm not a programmer or a designer. The keyword lists and rules are mine, written with AI. Nothing you read here is generated, but the site is made with AI, and that matters.

Why AI is a problem

A lot is wrong with it. Among other things:

  • It was trained on people's work without asking or paying them.
  • It uses a lot of energy and water.
  • A few huge companies control it.
  • And much more.

The one closest to home is designers and developers. A site like this is normally made by a designer and a developer, and I used neither, so that's paid work that didn't happen. But it's not only about jobs. These models learned from designers' and developers' work, mostly without their consent, and now they imitate it. They make design and code feel like something anyone can generate, and I can't judge the code or the design the way they could. What comes out also tends to look the same, so more of the web ends up looking alike. The more people do what I'm doing, the more of that there is.

Why I use it anyway

I couldn't build this otherwise. I can't code or design, and I can't pay anyone. I also couldn't find an independent, complete agenda for Amsterdam, and I think the city needs one. And I'm learning to use AI carefully, in the open. It's not a full answer, and I know.

What I do about it

  • AI builds the tool, never the content. Event texts come from the source.
  • I check every choice and result.
  • Light on energy: a simple page, updated twice a day, no servers running all day.
  • Every source is credited and linked.
  • Listed and want off? Tell me and it goes.
  • No profit, no ads.
  • The code is public.
  • I'm transparent and open to criticism.

Privacy and data

No cookies, no accounts, no tracking. I don't count visitors and I don't want to know.

Your filters, the English switch and your want-to-see list stay in your own browser. They never reach me.

Services your browser touches

  • GitHub hosts the site, so it sees your IP address like any host.
  • OpenStreetMap sends map tiles when you open a map.
  • Web3Forms receives what you type in the contact form, only when you press Send.
  • ics.agical.io builds the calendar file when you add an event. It gets the event name, venue, description and link, nothing else about you.

The form

The form uses a small service called Web3Forms. It passes your message, and your email if you add one, to my inbox. I keep messages only as long as I need to reply. Web3Forms has its own privacy policy.
